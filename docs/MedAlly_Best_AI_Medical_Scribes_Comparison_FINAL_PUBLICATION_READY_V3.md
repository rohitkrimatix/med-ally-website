## How We Compared AI Medical Scribes

A useful comparison should go beyond whether a tool can convert speech into a note.

We looked at six areas:

1. **Encounter capture and documentation:** Does the product support ambient or conversation-based note generation?
2. **Clinician review:** Is the output presented for clinician review and editing rather than treated as unquestioned final documentation?
3. **Customization and specialty fit:** Can documentation adapt to different note structures, specialties, or preferences?
4. **Beyond-the-note workflow:** Does the product extend into coding, evidence, clinical context, orders, pre-charting, follow-up, or related workflows?
5. **Integration:** Does the vendor describe EHR-connected or downstream workflow support?
6. **Pricing transparency:** Is current individual or practice pricing publicly available?

These criteria are not a universal scorecard. A solo physician and a multi-hospital health system may reasonably choose different products. Public documentation can also omit capabilities that may be available under specific contracts, integrations, or deployments.

## Which AI Medical Scribe Is Best for a Small Practice?

For a small practice, the most useful shortlist is usually different from the shortlist for a large health system.

Start by comparing:

- the monthly cost per clinician;
- whether there is a free plan or realistic trial;
- encounter or note limits;
- how quickly the tool can be adopted without a large implementation project;
- whether the generated note can be customized;
- whether you need coding or other post-note capabilities;
- and what happens after the clinician approves the note.

MedAlly, Freed, and Heidi all publish individual-access options or entry paths that make them relevant to investigate for smaller practices. That is an editorial shortlist based on public access and pricing information—not a performance ranking. The right choice still depends on the workflow you need rather than price alone.

## Which AI Medical Scribe Is Best for Specialist Physicians?

Specialists should look beyond generic SOAP-note generation.

Important questions include:

- Can the note structure adapt to the specialty?
- Does the system preserve relevant longitudinal context?
- Can it incorporate information needed before the encounter?
- Does it support the coding depth required by the specialty?
- Can the workflow accommodate complex assessments and plans?
- Is the EHR integration deep enough for the practice?

DeepScribe's current positioning is particularly specialty-focused. Abridge, Suki, Ambience, Nabla, Heidi, Freed, and MedAlly also describe different forms of specialty or workflow adaptability, so specialist buyers should evaluate the exact workflow rather than assume one product fits every specialty.

## What Should Physicians Compare Before Choosing an AI Medical Scribe?

### 1. The note you actually receive

Ask to see a real de-identified example. Look at structure, irrelevant content, missing context, and the amount of editing required.

### 2. Physician review and control

The system should make it clear what the clinician must review, approve, sign, or send forward.

### 3. Ambient capture versus dictation

Some physicians want the system to listen during the natural encounter. Others still need fast direct dictation. Some platforms support both.

### 4. What happens beyond documentation

If your workflow also requires coding, clinical context, patient instructions, orders, evidence, follow-up, or pre-charting, compare those capabilities separately rather than assuming every "AI scribe" includes them.

### 5. EHR workflow

"Integration" can mean very different things—from copying text into the chart to structured two-way workflows. Ask each vendor exactly what happens after the note is reviewed.

### 6. Pricing at your actual usage level

A low starting price can have encounter limits, feature restrictions, or higher-tier requirements. Compare the plan you would realistically use.

### 7. Product evidence and limitations

Ask how the vendor evaluates note quality, how limitations are communicated, and what happens when the system is uncertain or misses information.

## Comparison Limitations

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best AI medical scribe?

There is no universal best option. The right choice depends on practice size, specialty, note requirements, EHR workflow, pricing, and whether you need capabilities beyond documentation.

### What should I look for in an AI medical scribe?

Compare note quality and structure, clinician review controls, customization, specialty fit, EHR workflow, pricing, coding or other post-note capabilities, and the product's stated limitations.

### Are free AI medical scribes available?

Yes. Some vendors offer free plans or trials, but the limits vary. MedAlly's Forever Free plan includes 10 clinical encounters per month. Other vendors should be checked directly for their current free-plan or trial terms.

### Is an ambient AI scribe the same as an AI medical scribe?

The terms overlap heavily in the market. "Ambient" usually emphasizes capturing the natural patient-clinician conversation in the background of the encounter, while "AI medical scribe" is the broader product category used for software that helps generate clinical documentation.

### Should physicians review AI-generated notes?

Yes. AI-generated clinical documentation should be reviewed for accuracy, completeness, and appropriateness before it becomes part of the clinical record.

### Do all AI medical scribes integrate with EHRs the same way?

No. Integration depth varies substantially. Buyers should confirm whether a product copies text, pushes notes, works inside the EHR, supports structured fields, stages orders, or provides another workflow.

### Can an AI medical scribe help with coding?

Some platforms include coding support, but the scope differs by vendor and plan. Coding output should be reviewed and validated before use.
